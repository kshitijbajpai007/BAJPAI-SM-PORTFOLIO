CONCEPT ०२ · LINKEDIN ESSAY

The Cashback Dilemma & The Cross-Brand Loyalty Goldmine

A contrarian critique on reward psychology in Indian fintech, consumer autonomy, and the untapped multi-brand loyalty architecture.

क्ष
Kshitij Bajpai · 1st
Content & Brand Strategist | B.Tech CSE @ Galgotias
Strategic Specimen · 🌐 Public
FINTECH CONSUMER PSYCHOLOGY

Google Pay screwed up their cashback rewards system and it revealed everything that’s wrong with the rewards and loyalty programs across India.

Back when GPay was new, they were generous in their cashbacks, and gained lots of users just with that USP.

But later, they started giving out completely unnecessary coupons of teeth straighteners and such.

And one thing they forgot to consider, is that:
Consumers love to choose.

They want to choose what dessert they can have after a heavy dinner.
They want to see all the available options just to choose from one of them.
(Amazon literally built a campaign years back on this, ‘Aur Dikhao’ and it did wonders.)

So being forced a reward is like a death sentence to a consumer.
And that scratch card after each payment on GPay is just that.
A useless voucher which everyone knows will expire.

Zomato did something really clever for this.
Even though half their coupons feel unnecessary. (apart from the Audible and Lenskart one)
But the way they hand them out is brilliant.

A fortune wheel with coupons all over, and some algorithms choose it for you.
Or sometimes, you get to choose from one of the 4 available coupons.
So the blame lands on either your luck or your choice altogether.
Zomato fulfills its brand partnerships.
And you feel like you chose this.

But there is still one thing India has not completely discovered:
True to God cross-promotion across all brands and verticals.

Imagine, points I earn at Croma can get me something at Vijay Sales.
Or better, coffee stamps at Starbucks can get me some free cookie at Blue Tokai.

Such a dynamic access-to-all rewards program will revolutionize how Indian consumers get treated for their loyalty towards a brand.

It’s a Hail Mary,
But the first person to crack this code is sitting on a gold mine.

Strategic Breakdown:
Hook: Contrarian teardown of India's ubiquitous fintech habit (GPay) Psychology: Autonomy Bias & The Illusion of Choice (Amazon 'Aur Dikhao') Case Contrast: Gamification vs Friction (Zomato's algorithmic wheel) Vision: Interoperable loyalty ecosystem across competing retail verticals

SCRIPT 01 · PERSONAL GROWTH & PSYCHOLOGY

"Four Friends You Need"

⏱ ~60-70s Spoken Pacing
🎣 MULTI-ANGLE HOOK MATRIX (TESTED FOR A/B RETENTION)
VERBAL SPOKEN HOOKS (FIRST 3 SECONDS):
HOOK 1 · CONTROVERSIAL

"If you have more than 4 best friends, I'm already judging you. Because there are only 4 kinds of friends you need to grow without feeling alone in your twenties."

HOOK 2 · BENEFIT-DRIVEN

"4 kinds of friends you need to make the most of your twenties."

HOOK 3 · CONTRARIAN

"Stop expecting one friend to be everything for you. You need 4 totally different friends, and each one has a very different role in your life."

ON-SCREEN TEXT HOOKS (THUMBNAIL & 0–2s OVERLAY):
Text Hook 1: 4 Friends Every Woman Needs Text Hook 2: You Don't Need A Big Friend Group Text Hook 3: 4 Friends That Make Your 20s Better
SPOKEN SCRIPT & RETENTION BEATS:
TYPE 1 · THE EXAMPLE

This friend is already living the kind of life you want. And she's proof that what you're dreaming of is actually possible.

She's made the mistakes you're scared of making. So instead of figuring everything out from scratch, you get to learn from someone who's already been there.

TYPE 2 · THE BUDDY

This is the friend who holds you to your own standards. If you said you'd start going to the gym, she's calling you at 7 AM. If you said you'd apply for a new job, she's asking if you've sent that application.
